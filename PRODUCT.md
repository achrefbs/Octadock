# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Windows developers, designers, technical founders, support engineers, analysts,
and creators who repeatedly turn screenshots, logs, documentation, dictated
notes, and other on-screen material into usable work. They value speed and
polish, but do not want passive screen monitoring or surprise uploads.

## Product Purpose

Octadock is the local-first capture-to-context workspace for Windows. It turns
anything on screen into useful, reviewable context through an explicit flow:
capture or dictate, inspect or transform, keep on the Shelf or in Context, then
copy, export, or deliberately hand off. The website should make that outcome and
the product's precise local/network boundaries immediately understandable.

## Brand Personality

Calm, direct, and engineered. Precise and privacy-aware without becoming cold;
builder-focused without dressing generic SaaS conventions up as technical
credibility. The Aquarium is atmospheric and distinctive, while the foreground
content remains legible, honest, and practical.

## Anti-references

- Generic feature-count screenshot utilities and interchangeable SaaS landing
  pages that lead with a card grid instead of the capture-to-context outcome.
- Ambient AI mission-control dashboards, passive screen-watching stories, or
  copy that obscures when data crosses a boundary.
- Mascot treatment of the octopus: eyes, faces, biological tentacles, suction
  cups, or character animation in the identity mark.
- Cloud-first marketing, invented availability, or claims that local-first means
  fully offline.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the outcome: turn the screen into context, not a toolbox inventory.
2. Explain trust through mechanics and name every network boundary precisely.
3. Keep the Aquarium expressive while semantic content remains normal,
   foreground DOM that never depends on WebGL or JavaScript.
4. Preserve the engineered eight-input identity and the rule “dock, don't swim.”
5. Treat Beta labels and release placeholders as scope promises, not marketing
   gaps to hide.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. The complete site must remain readable and operable with
JavaScript or WebGL unavailable, at narrow viewports and enlarged text, from the
keyboard, and with visible focus. Honor reduced motion, forced colors, and coarse
pointers; decorative canvas, custom cursor, and continuous motion must never
block content or controls.
