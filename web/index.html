Local-first, on the record

"Private" is easy to say. Here is the complete network story instead, and every claim on this page maps to shipped code.

Your captures never leave your PC

Screenshots, recordings, OCR text, history, and clipboard entries are stored locally. Octadock does not upload them.

Dictation needs a one-time model download

The first time you dictate, Octadock downloads a speech model from Hugging Face. It runs on your PC after that. You are shown the size and can cancel.

"Explain" and "summarize" use your own AI CLI

Read-aloud can first run text through the Codex or Claude CLI you already have installed. That is a network call to your AI provider, billed by them, and it stays off unless you use those verbs.

Optional OpenAI & ElevenLabs are opt-in

Cloud speech-to-text (OpenAI) and cloud voices (ElevenLabs) are off by default. When you enable them with your own key, that provider handles the request and bills you.

Capture, annotation, OCR, read-aloud with Windows voices, file preview, pins, clipboard history, and the text tools make no network calls. Screen recording records video only; audio capture isn't built yet, so we don't sell it. The local Context Stack bundles captures and files into an exportable package; its AI/MCP/redaction layer is in development and ships to Local licenses at no extra cost. This website makes no network calls either: no analytics, no external fonts, no trackers. Full detail on the privacy page.

Scriptable to the bone

A real CLI, octadock:// protocol URLs, global hotkeys, and file associations. Wire captures into scripts, AutoHotkey, or your agent tooling. Commands are validated locally and handed to the running tray app over a per-user local pipe; nothing listens on the network.

PowerShell
# grab a region straight to the clipboard
> octadock capture-area --action copy

# OCR a fixed region as lines
> octadock ocr --area 100,120,800,600 --mode lines

# speak whatever you copied
> octadock read --clipboard
